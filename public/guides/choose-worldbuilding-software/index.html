How to choose worldbuilding software

Decide what you need to track, then check each tool for those features, how it charges, and whether you can export your work. Test with a free plan or trial before paying.

1. Decide whether the manuscript lives in the same tool

Some tools focus on world reference, some on drafting, and some combine both. If you want to see your notes while you write, an all-in-one workspace saves switching. If you already have a drafting tool you love, a reference-only tool may be enough. Neither is wrong; know which you are choosing.

2. List the things you must track

Write down what your story really needs: characters and relationships, family trees, timelines, maps, a lore encyclopedia, factions, notes. Then check each tool against your list rather than a feature count. A tool with fewer features that covers your list beats one with many that misses your key need.

3. Test the hard cases

Basic character sheets are easy. Test what is difficult for your story: step-families and adoptions in a family tree, events spanning centuries on a timeline, a map with hundreds of places. Try these in a free plan before paying.

4. Understand the pricing model

Check whether you are paying monthly, annually or once, and what stops working if you stop paying. Ask: do I keep access to my projects? Can I still open the app? Is cloud sync a separate charge? A one-time licence and a subscription can both be good value, but they carry different risks.

5. Check data ownership and exports

You should be able to leave. Look for export to common formats (DOCX, PDF) and a full backup you can restore. Check whether projects can be stored on your own device as well as in the cloud.

6. Check platform support

Confirm it runs where you write: browser, desktop, or both, offline or online only. If you write on a phone or tablet, check explicitly, because many tools are designed for desktop.

7. Look at how the product is maintained

Look for a recent release, a changelog and a way to reach the maker. Tools built by one person can be excellent, but check they are being updated.

8. Try before you pay

Move a small real project in. How the tool feels on your own material tells you more than a feature table.
