How to track characters across a book series

Keep one record per character, record facts as they become canon, and note what changes between books so you can check each new scene against it.

1. One record per character

Keep a single profile for each character with the fixed facts: name and aliases, age and birth date, appearance, voice, background and motivation. When the series passes through several books, the profile is your source of truth.

2. Separate fixed facts from changing ones

Eye colour does not change; a character's allegiance, injuries or relationships might. Record changing facts with the book or scene where they change, so you can answer "what did she know at this point?"

3. Track relationships, not just people

A character's relationships drive scenes. Map who trusts, loves, fears or owes whom, and update the map when a book changes it. A relationship map shows quickly when a friendship you have written as warm is still marked as hostile.

4. Keep a first-appearance log

Note the book and chapter where each character first appears and what readers have been told. This prevents characters knowing things they could not have learned yet.

5. Watch the timeline

Characters age. If three books cover twenty years, check ages against the timeline each time. A birth date and a dated list of events make arithmetic errors visible.

6. Check before each book

Before drafting a new book, re-read the profiles of every returning character and the relationship map. Fix gaps before they reach the page.

7. Record decisions you make while drafting

When you invent a detail mid-scene, such as a scar or a favourite drink, add it to the profile straight away. Details invented during drafting are the ones most likely to be forgotten.
