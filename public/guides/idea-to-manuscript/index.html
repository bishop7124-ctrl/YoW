From idea to manuscript: a novel planning workflow

Capture the idea, write a one-paragraph premise, outline in beats, build only the world the story needs, then draft scene by scene and revise.

1. Capture ideas without judging them

Keep one place for loose ideas: scenes, characters, images, lines of dialogue. Do not sort them yet. An ideas board works because it is cheap to add to and easy to scan later.

2. Write the premise in a paragraph

Who wants what, what stands in the way, and what is at stake? If you cannot say it in a paragraph, the idea needs more thought before you outline.

3. Outline in beats

List the major turning points: the opening situation, the event that starts the story, the midpoint shift, the low moment, the climax and the resolution. Use whatever structure suits you; the point is to know where the story is heading.

4. Build only the world you need

Create the characters and places the outline requires. Fill in lore as scenes demand it rather than all in advance, which is a common way for planning to replace writing.

5. Break the outline into chapters and scenes

Give each scene a purpose: what changes in it? Scenes with no change are candidates for cutting or combining. A word target per chapter helps you judge pacing.

6. Draft, then revise

Write the first draft without editing. Track progress against a daily or weekly goal. Revise afterwards for structure first, then scenes, then sentences.

7. Keep notes and the manuscript close

Facts you invent while drafting should go into your notes straight away, so the draft and the reference stay in agreement.

8. Export and share

When a draft is ready for readers or an editor, export it in a format they can open, such as DOCX or PDF.
